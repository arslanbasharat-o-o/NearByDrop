How to send from Google Pixel to Mac via Quick Share:

  1. Ensure both your Mac and Pixel are connected to the same Wi-Fi router or phone mobile hotspot.
  2. Launch Nearby Drop on your Mac (it sits quietly in your menu bar).
  3. On your Pixel, open Photos or Files, select your files, and tap Share → Quick Share.
  4. Your Mac will automatically appear under available devices. Tap it to send.
  5. Your Mac displays a confirmation with matching 6-digit PIN. Click Accept and files land in ~/Downloads/.
// 02 PROTOCOL ARCHITECTURE

How Google Nearby Share Works on Mac

Nearby Drop reverse-engineers the official Google Quick Share wire protocol to communicate directly with Android's system share sheet.

[ 01 / DISCOVERY ]

mDNS Beacon

Nearby Drop advertises the Google Nearby Share service (_FC9F5ED42C8A._tcp.local.) over local network multicast DNS. Your Mac automatically appears in Android’s native Quick Share sheet.

DISCOVERY: Zero Cloud Servers
[ 02 / HANDSHAKE ]

UKEY2 Verification

A secure handshake runs using Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman (P-256). Both devices independently derive a 6-digit confirmation PIN to protect against man-in-the-middle attacks before any files are accepted.

SECURITY: ECDH P-256 + HKDF
[ 03 / TRANSFER ]

Direct TCP Sockets

Payload data streams directly from your Android phone across your local Wi-Fi router straight into your Mac's network buffer. Typical real-world throughput is 25–80 MB/s on 5GHz 802.11ac/ax Wi-Fi.

THROUGHPUT: Raw Local LAN Line Rate
[ 04 / PERSISTENCE ]

Native Finder Save

Transferred photos, 4K videos, and zip files are written directly into your ~/Downloads/ directory. An alert sound plays with a one-click button to reveal files in Finder.

DESTINATION: Direct to Downloads

Nearby Share & Near Drop for Mac

Nearby Drop is an enhanced open-source macOS menu bar application based on the Google Quick Share / Nearby Share protocol (commonly sought after by users searching for NearDrop or Near Drop). It provides seamless receiver support for Android-to-Mac transfers over Wi-Fi and adds a native USB-C MTP storage manager for wired transfers.
Because Apple doesn't make AirDrop available for Android, Google built Quick Share (formerly Nearby Share). With Nearby Drop running on your Mac, your Mac appears directly inside your Android phone's Quick Share sheet. Select your files on your phone, tap Quick Share, tap your Mac, and confirm the matching PIN on your screen.
Unlike marketing hype claiming theoretical gigabit speeds, real-world local Wi-Fi transfers typically operate between 25 MB/s and 80 MB/s on standard 5GHz Wi-Fi routers. A 1 GB 4K video takes approximately 15–30 seconds. Over direct USB-C cable using the built-in MTP manager, transfers run at high hardware speeds (~80–150 MB/s).
Nearby Drop is an independent community open-source tool. We do not pay Apple $99/year for an official Developer ID certificate. The app is completely ad-hoc signed and 100% public on GitHub. On first run, simply right-click the app in /Applications, select Open, and confirm, or allow it in macOS System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway.
