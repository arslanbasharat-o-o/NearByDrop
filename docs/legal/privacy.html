Privacy

Draft for project-owner and legal review. This page describes behavior observed in the published site and current app source; hosting-provider practices are controlled by the provider.

Website visits

The Nearby Drop site is static and hosted by GitHub Pages. Its source does not add analytics tags, advertising pixels, sign-in, contact forms, or tracking cookies. The site saves your appearance choice (System, Light, or Dark) in this browser’s local storage under nearby-drop-theme; that preference stays on your device and is not sent to Nearby Drop.

GitHub operates the hosting service and may process connection details such as IP address and request information under its privacy statement. The project owner cannot set GitHub’s log retention or independently verify its internal handling. Links to GitHub, Google, and other services open those services under their own privacy terms.

Information stored by the macOS app

Nearby Drop stores app preferences on the Mac. These include appearance and accent choices, the selected download folder, the device name, trusted-device entries, and transfer history. The app keeps up to 20 recent transfer records. A record can include a file name, size, transfer direction, peer name, timestamp, and local file URL. You can clear transfer history and remove trusted devices in the app.

Files are saved to the folder you choose. Wireless transfers connect directly between nearby devices over a reachable local network; USB MTP uses the connected cable. The app does not relay transferred files through a Nearby Drop cloud service. Treat trusted-device settings and live pairing codes as sensitive because they affect who can initiate or automatically receive transfers.

Update checks and external services

The app checks GitHub for signed update metadata at launch and periodically while it is running; the current updater configuration disables sending a system profile. The app also uses the Quick Share protocol and QR workflows described in the protocol notes.

When the Mac displays a Quick Share QR code, its link opens Google’s hosted flow at quickshare.google and contains a temporary connection key in the URL fragment. Google operates that external flow; its handling is outside the project’s control. This is separate from scanning an Android-generated QR code in Nearby Drop, which starts the app’s local receiving workflow. Treat displayed QR codes as temporary connection credentials and do not publish them.

The app also writes troubleshooting events to macOS unified logging. The app marks log message content as private, and it does not automatically send these logs to the project. macOS controls local diagnostic log retention.
