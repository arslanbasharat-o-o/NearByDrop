# Positioning, launch material and a 30-day experiment

Status: drafts only. Nothing posted, emailed, deployed or published. Research checked November 22, 2026 against primary sources below; capabilities can change.

## Positioning

Nearby Drop is a native Mac menu bar utility for compatible Quick Share transfers and Android USB file management. The upgrade combines a clear connection-recovery path, fast recipient selection, explicit content sharing and original tropical appearances. Themes are a promotional hook to test, not evidence of retention or a compatibility guarantee.

Audience hypotheses: Android + Mac owners who want to keep their existing phone sharing workflow; people who regularly move photos/documents over a cable; mixed Mac/Windows/Android teams. Ask testers which workflow actually brought them here before committing to a segment.

## Alternatives, without invented exclusivity

| Product | Officially described capabilities | Practical comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [LocalSend](https://localsend.org/) | Cross-platform local-network sharing; install it on participating devices; files and text | A credible alternative when installing a companion on each device is acceptable. Nearby Drop targets compatible existing Quick Share workflows and offers MTP too. |
| [KDE Connect](https://kdeconnect.kde.org/) | Files/links plus broader device integration such as notifications and remote controls | Broader integration can be attractive; this project stays focused on sharing and USB. Do not claim feature parity or macOS-specific support from the general feature page. |
| [OpenMTP](https://openmtp.ganeshrvel.com/) | Android/MTP file management on macOS with a split pane and storage switching | A focused USB alternative. Our current-folder filter/sort is useful but is not an equivalent dual-pane browser. |
| [NearDrop](https://github.com/grishka/NearDrop) | Unofficial Quick Share implementation for macOS, with documented discovery limitations | A closely related compatibility space. Avoid claims of universal discovery or that sending is unique to Nearby Drop. |

Do not repeat vendor speed claims as independent benchmarks. No head-to-head hardware benchmark was performed here.

## Release announcement draft

Nearby Drop's next candidate brings three original appearances—Vice Nights, Coastal Sunrise and Midnight Route—to a native Mac sharing utility. Connection Doctor explains what the Mac can observe and offers useful QR/USB next steps. Favorite recipients make selection quicker without bypassing approval. You can prepare text or links as UTF-8 files, review a screenshot before sharing, and filter or sort a phone's current USB folder.

The candidate requires macOS 14+, includes Apple silicon and Intel binary slices, and retains the 128-file wireless limit. Compatibility depends on the device/client/network. Build 23 sends diagnostics automatically after its setup disclosure, without in-app reporting switches. The collector/dashboard are deployed. This is independent original artwork, with no Google/Apple/Rockstar affiliation. Signing/notarization and hardware verification status must accompany the approved download.

## Demonstration storyboard (45 seconds)

1. 0–5s: identify a Mac + Android workflow; show the actual app, not a fake transfer.
2. 5–12s: choose a file, pick a real device, show approval on the phone. Record only after a physical transfer passes.
3. 12–20s: show the three appearances and immediate switching, keeping the same controls visible.
4. 20–28s: show Connection Doctor's observed-vs-suggested guidance; demonstrate a real QR route if verified.
5. 28–36s: preview explicit text-file/screenshot sharing and a USB current-folder filter. Label fixture/demo data if used.
6. 36–45s: show compatibility, the approved release/download and privacy controls. Do not add fake usage counts, fake speed figures or an unverified successful phone transfer.

## Four-week execution plan

| Week | Concrete work | Leading indicators / decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Recruit 5–8 voluntary testers across a few Android models; record environment, first-transfer result and friction manually; complete signing/hardware gates | First successful transfer per tested setup; recurring discovery/install failure categories; prioritize the most repeated blocker |
| 2 | Refine the help/installation copy from those observations; record the demonstration with approved content; prepare one Mac-focused and one Android-focused announcement | Help-page questions answered; time to first success in small observed tests; avoid projecting these samples to all users |
| 3 | After publication authorization, release the candidate with accurate verification status; share in a small number of relevant communities where the product is useful | Public GitHub asset download deltas, voluntary feedback, reproductions; downloads are not people or active installations |
| 4 | Compare feedback from themed/default experiences; fix the highest-impact failure; decide whether favorite/doctor workflows warrant deeper investment | Repeat voluntary use, meaningful feedback, and—under the disclosed automatic-reporting policy—eligible operational cohorts |

Theme test: ask consenting testers which appearance they choose and why; preserve function/installation instructions across variants. With current small usage, report counts and uncertainty rather than claiming statistically significant lift. No new website trackers, fingerprints, background clipboard access or operational rollout are needed for this preparatory plan.

## Download and activation measurement

Keep public asset download counts, random reporting installation IDs, active reporting IDs and actual observed successful users separate. Legacy completions lack an attempt denominator. The new dashboard shows cohort definitions, retries, missing starts, pending and unknown, but remains gated. A shareable issue template should ask for version, OS/model category, method and sanitized error category; never solicit file contents or live pairing codes.
