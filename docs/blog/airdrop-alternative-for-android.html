The Best AirDrop Alternative for Android & Mac in 2026

By Arslan Basharat · 4 min read

AirDrop is arguably one of the greatest features in the Apple ecosystem. The ability to tap a file and have it wirelessly beam at high speed to another device without manual pairing or cloud uploads is a marvel of consumer engineering. But what happens when you daily drive an Android phone alongside a MacBook or Mac mini?

You hit an artificial wall. Apple maintains AirDrop as an exclusive walled-garden feature. Android users are usually told to use clunky alternatives like Bluetooth (which transfers at an agonizing 1 MB/s), cloud storage (which burns mobile data and cloud storage space), or third-party web relays that require scanning temporary browser links.

Why Nearbydrop is Different

Rather than inventing yet another proprietary protocol that forces you to install an unfamiliar mobile app on your phone, Nearbydrop takes advantage of the protocol already built into your Android phone: Quick Share (formerly Nearby Share).

  • Native Phone Experience: You use the native Android Share sheet. Tap Quick Share and your Mac appears in the device list. No extra phone companion app needed.
  • Full Wi-Fi 6 Hardware Speeds: Nearbydrop streams files across local TCP sockets over your Wi-Fi router or hotspot, achieving up to 500+ MB/s. Transferring a 4 GB 4K video takes seconds.
  • Native macOS Integration: Rather than running in a heavy web browser or Electron wrapper, Nearbydrop is built natively for macOS in lightweight SwiftUI, sitting quietly in your menu bar.
  • Built-in USB MTP Mode: When Wi-Fi is unavailable or when managing hundreds of gigabytes of raw files, Nearbydrop includes a high-performance MTP engine to browse phone storage and drag-and-drop straight into Finder.

Comparison: How It Stacks Up

Here is how Nearbydrop compares to other common solutions:

  • Apple AirDrop: Incredible between Macs and iPhones, but completely non-functional with Android.
  • Cloud Storage (Google Drive / Dropbox): Requires waiting for double uploads/downloads, compresses video files on certain platforms, and runs out of free space quickly.
  • Bluetooth File Sharing: Maxes out at around 1–2 MB/s; completely impractical for videos or photo albums.
  • Nearbydrop: Direct peer-to-peer Wi-Fi at hardware speed, zero cloud dependency, zero subscriptions.
