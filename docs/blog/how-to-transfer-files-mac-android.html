How to Transfer Files from Android to Mac Fast (No Cloud, Zero Lag)

By Arslan Basharat · Updated for macOS 14 & 15 · 5 min read

Transferring files between an Android smartphone and a Mac has notoriously been one of the biggest friction points in modern computing. Apple's AirDrop exclusively works across Apple devices, while Google's legacy "Android File Transfer" utility has been discontinued and often crashes with modern Android releases.

Uploading gigabytes of 4K video clips or photo archives to Google Drive, Dropbox, or messaging apps takes forever, compresses image quality, and eats into cloud storage quotas.

Here is how to achieve true local peer-to-peer file transfers up to 500+ MB/s between Android and macOS without third-party cloud servers using Nearbydrop.

Method 1: Wireless Transfer via Quick Share (Fastest & Most Convenient)

Modern Android phones (Samsung, Google Pixel, Xiaomi, OnePlus, Motorola, Sony) come pre-installed with Google’s Quick Share protocol. Nearbydrop brings this protocol natively into the macOS menu bar.

Step 1: Download and install Nearbydrop on your Mac

Grab the universal installer DMG from GitHub Releases and drag Nearbydrop into your /Applications directory:

Download Nearbydrop (.DMG)

Step 2: Connect to the same local Wi-Fi

Ensure both your Mac and Android smartphone are connected to the same Wi-Fi network. If you are outdoors or traveling, simply turn on your phone's personal hotspot and connect your Mac to it—Nearbydrop works 100% offline without requiring internet data.

Step 3: Select files on Android and tap Quick Share

  1. Open your phone's Gallery or Files app and select the photos, videos, or folders you want to send.
  2. Tap the Share button and choose Quick Share.
  3. Your Mac running Nearbydrop will immediately pop up in the list of available devices. Tap it to send.

Step 4: Accept on your Mac

Nearbydrop’s menu bar icon will alert you of an incoming transfer with a preview of the filenames, size, and a matching PIN code. Click Accept. The files will stream across your local network at full hardware speeds (often 300–500 MB/s on Wi-Fi 6) directly into your Mac's Downloads folder.

Method 2: Inline QR Code Pairing (Instant Direct Connect)

Nearbydrop includes a built-in optical QR camera scanner:

  • Tap Quick Share on your Android phone and select Share with QR Code.
  • Click Nearbydrop in the Mac menu bar and choose Receive → Scan QR.
  • Point your Mac's built-in webcam at the screen; Nearbydrop reads the cryptographic session token and negotiates an encrypted connection on the fly.

Method 3: Direct USB-C Cable (MTP Storage Browser)

If you have hundreds of gigabytes of raw footage or want to browse your phone's internal storage like a drive:

  1. Connect your phone with a USB-C cable.
  2. On your phone, change USB settings to File transfer / Android Auto.
  3. Open Nearbydrop and select the USB tab to browse internal memory and SD cards, download directories, or drag and drop files from Finder.

🔒 Why Zero-Cloud Matters

Every wireless transfer uses the UKEY2 cryptographic handshake with elliptic-curve Diffie-Hellman keys. No telemetry, account credentials, or file bytes are ever uploaded to remote servers.

Summary

You no longer need slow Bluetooth, cumbersome cables, or costly cloud storage subscriptions to move files between Android and macOS. Nearbydrop makes cross-platform file transfers feel as effortless as native AirDrop.
